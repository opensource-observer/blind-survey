## Rules: statement style

Every statement drafted for approval obeys all of these.

1. One complete thought per statement. Someone reading it with no other context
   must understand it. No "this" or "that" pointing at something outside it.
2. Plain, standard English in a neutral voice. Style is a fingerprint, so
   rewrite the speaker's words: drop unusual vocabulary, favorite phrases,
   metaphors, and sentence rhythm. Never carry a memorable phrase through word
   for word, however short.
3. Confidence lives in the opening words and nowhere else: "I'm confident that…"
   for high, "I believe…" for medium, "My hunch is…" for low. Match how they
   hedged: "I'm confident" is only for what they asserted flatly. "I believe" is
   for something they hold but would not be surprised to be wrong about. "My
   hunch is" is for a guess they offer tentatively. Never a number, never a
   percentage.
4. Never include any of these:
   - names, job titles, or roles
   - dates, date ranges, or references to when something happened ("next year",
     "last quarter", "since March")
   - direct quotes
   - internal project names few people would recognize
   - references to a particular meeting or document

   Rewrite time-anchored wording into something durable: "next year's funding
   round" becomes "the near-term funding round". A time horizon that is the
   point of the statement ("within three years") is fine.
5. Every statement should be one at least two people in this group could have
   said. If it couldn't, say so during approval.
