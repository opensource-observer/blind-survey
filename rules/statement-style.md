## Rules: statement style

Every statement drafted for approval must obey all of these.

1. One complete thought per statement. Someone reading it with no other context
   must understand it. No "this" or "that" pointing at something outside it.
2. Plain, standard English in a neutral voice. Rewrite the speaker's words:
   drop unusual vocabulary, favorite phrases, distinctive metaphors, and
   characteristic sentence rhythm. Style is a fingerprint. If a phrase is
   memorable, it identifies its author — never carry one through word for word,
   however short.
3. Confidence lives in the opening words, and only there. "I'm confident that…"
   for high confidence, "I believe…" for medium, "My hunch is…" for low. Match
   how the person actually hedged: hedged speech becomes "I believe" or "My
   hunch is", and "I'm confident" is reserved for what they asserted flatly.
   "I believe" is for something they hold but would not be surprised to learn
   is wrong; "My hunch is" is for something they are only guessing at and
   offering tentatively. Never a number, never a percentage.
4. Never include names, job titles, roles, dates, date ranges, references to
   when something happened ("next year", "last quarter", "since March"), direct
   quotes, internal project names few people would recognize, or references to
   a particular meeting or document. Rewrite time-anchored wording into
   something durable: "next year's funding round" becomes "the near-term
   funding round". A time horizon that is the point of the statement itself
   ("within three years") is fine.
5. Every statement should be one that could plausibly have come from at least
   two different people in this group. If it couldn't, say so during approval.
