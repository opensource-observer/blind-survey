# {{ survey.title }} — what this is, and what happens to what you say

You are being asked to spend about {{ survey.duration_minutes }} minutes in a

About {{ survey.anonymity.expected_respondents }} people are being asked the same
thing, which is the group you are anonymous within.

## The five things worth knowing

1. **The conversation is yours.** It runs in your own model account. Nobody
   organizing this can see it, then or later.
2. **You decide what leaves it.** At the end the interviewer writes short,
   neutral statements of what you said, and you go through them one at a time:
   keep, edit, or cut. Nothing is sent automatically.
3. **You do the submitting.** What you kept arrives as a block of text you paste
   into an anonymous form. The form asks for nothing else — no name, no email,
   no login. You can submit more than once if you think of something later.
4. **The grouping goes away, but not right away.** The statements you kept
   arrive together, in the same file, so whoever handles the raw submissions
   could see which ones arrived together. The first pass breaks that file
   into separate statements and records nothing about which arrived
   together; the second pass — the one reading for what people meant —
   shuffles the list again and never opens the raw file at all. By the time
   anyone is interpreting your words, nothing connects them to each other
   anymore.
5. **The limits, honestly.** Something only you would know still points at you,
   whatever channel carries it. This conversation is subject to your model
   account's own retention policy, whatever that policy turns out to say. The
   protection that actually works is the step where you approve each statement,
   which is why it happens one at a time.

What follows is not our summary of the rules — it is the exact text the
interviewer is given, unedited, so you can check it yourself rather than take
our word for it.

## What comes back

The statements from everyone are grouped: where people agree, where they
contradict each other, and where the same word turns out to mean different
things to different people. Disagreements stay disagreements. Nothing is
attributed.
