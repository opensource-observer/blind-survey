## Five things worth knowing

1. **The conversation is yours.** It runs in your own model account. Nobody
   organizing this can see it, then or later.
2. **You decide what leaves it.** At the end the interviewer writes short,
   neutral statements of what you said. You go through them one at a time: keep,
   edit, or cut. Nothing is sent automatically.
3. **You do the submitting.** What you kept comes back as a block of text you
   paste into an anonymous form. The form asks for nothing else: no name, no
   email, no login. You can submit again later if you think of something.
4. **The grouping goes away, but not right away.** Your kept statements arrive
   together in one file, so whoever handles the raw submissions could see which
   ones arrived together. The first pass splits that file into separate
   statements, recording nothing about the grouping. The second pass, which
   reads for what people meant, shuffles them again and never opens the raw
   file. Nothing then connects your statements to each other.
5. **The limits, honestly.** Something only you would know still points at you,
   whatever channel carries it. This conversation is subject to your model
   account's retention policy, whatever that says. The protection that works is
   your approval of each statement, one at a time.

## What comes back

Everyone's statements are grouped: where people agree, where they contradict
each other, and where the same word means different things to different people.
Disagreements stay disagreements. Nothing is attributed.
